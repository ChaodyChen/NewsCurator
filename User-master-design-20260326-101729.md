## Problem Statement

Your company's leadership and key teams rely on curated semiconductor market & technical news to inform strategic decisions and stay competitive. Currently, you're using an existing news aggregation tool that has three critical failures:

1. **Broken links** — URLs sometimes point to dead pages or wrong articles

The core insight from your research: **The bottleneck is curation, not search.** Finding semiconductor news is easy. Knowing which 5 matter most is hard.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Manual Curation Pipeline
**Summary:** Minimal MVP. Daily fetch from news APIs → you rank manually → top 5 auto-sent Monday 8am.
**Effort:** Small | **Risk:** Low

**Pros:**
- Simplest to ship (week 1)
- No infrastructure (just script + cron)
- Transparent and auditable

**Cons:**
- You still rank 20-30 stories manually every week
- No learning mechanism (can't improve next week automatically)
- Doesn't scale if volume grows

---

### Approach B: Hybrid with Smart Pre-Filtering ← **RECOMMENDED**
**Summary:** Daily fetch → keyword + heuristic filtering narrows to ~15 candidates → you rank those → top 5 sent Monday 8am. Store selections in database. After 4-8 weeks, train scoring model on your preferences.
**Effort:** Medium | **Risk:** Medium

**Pros:**
- Reduces your weekly curation from 30 stories to ~15 (50% savings)
- **Learning dataset builds automatically** — every week you're labeling training data
- **Clear path to automation** — by month 2, model can pre-score, you just approve
- Matches your stated need: "system learns my selection principles"

**Cons:**
- Requires database + initial heuristics (keyword lists, source weighting)
- Heuristics may be wrong initially; will need tuning
- Takes ~2 weeks to deliver vs. 1 week for Approach A

---

### Approach C: Cross-Verified Redundancy
**Summary:** Fetch from 5+ sources in parallel → cross-verify stories → only stories appearing in 2+ sources bubble up → you rank verified candidates → top 5 sent.
**Effort:** Medium-Large | **Risk:** Medium-High

**Pros:**
- Fact-checking is built into the data layer (multiple sources = truth signal)
- Catches broken links before delivery
- Very high-quality starting point

**Cons:**
- Complex aggregation logic + rate-limiting headaches
- Overkill for phase 1 (can add in phase 2 as a fact-check layer)

## Recommended Approach: Approach B

**Why:** It's the right complexity level. Simple enough to ship in 2 weeks, ambitious enough to teach us how to build the learning system. Most importantly, **every week you curate, you're automatically creating training data for the ML model** — no extra effort.

## Operational Resilience

**Fact-checking:**
- HEAD request to each URL (5-second timeout)
- If timeout, story is kept but flagged as "Link unverified" in curation UI (doesn't auto-filter)
- Verified again at Monday delivery time
- If link is dead at delivery, story is dropped and replaced with backup candidate

**LINE Delivery:**
- Primary: LINE Bot API
- Fallback: Email alert to ops if LINE API fails
- Retry strategy: Attempt delivery every 5 minutes for 1 hour
- Logging: All delivery attempts logged for audit

**Curation Timing:**
- Candidates ready by Thu 6am
- Deadline: You must select by Sunday 10pm
- Fallback: If no selections by Sunday 10pm, system uses last week's top 5 (re-verified) and sends alert

**Database:**
- PostgreSQL (even free tier AWS RDS) to avoid SQLite concurrent-write locking
- Backup daily snapshots to S3
- All selections stored with timestamp + user for audit trail

## APPENDIX: Design Rationale

### Why Approach B (Hybrid with Smart Pre-Filtering)?

We rejected Approach A (manual-only) because you'd spend 30-60 minutes weekly ranking 20-30 stories with no system improvement. Approach A is defensible if this is a one-week sprint, but you said you want the system to learn your judgment. That's ambitious and worth the extra complexity.

We rejected Approach C (cross-verified redundancy) because it's overkill for Phase 1. It solves a real problem (fact-checking), but you're already willing to manually spot-check links. Cross-verification can be added later as a data quality layer.

Approach B is the Goldilocks choice: it reduces your workload by 50%, and **every week you curate, you're automatically creating training data for the ML model.** There's no extra work. By week 8, you have 8 weeks of labeled data and can train a model that partially automates the ranking.

### Why PostgreSQL, not SQLite?

SQLite is simpler (single file), but concurrent writes from scheduled cron jobs can cause locking issues, especially under contention. PostgreSQL is more robust and handles the concurrency naturally. The extra operational overhead is minimal; even AWS RDS free tier is sufficient for Phase 1.

### Why Operational Fallbacks Matter

This tool will be used by your leadership team. If Monday 8am arrives and no message appears, it erodes trust immediately. Fallback logic (email alert, last week's top 5, retry logic) ensures the system degrades gracefully rather than failing silently.
